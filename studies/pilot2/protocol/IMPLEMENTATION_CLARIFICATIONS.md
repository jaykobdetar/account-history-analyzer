# Pre-score implementation conventions

These conventions make underspecified engineering details in the registered text explicit before any new performance score. They do not alter the original registration, source histories, measurement definitions, default guards or primary outcomes. The tested adapters and these conventions are bound by the final scoring freeze.

For chronological preparation, a source-account median and the union-median cut use the upper middle supplied timestamp after chronological sorting. Thus an even-sized sequence uses index `n // 2`, not the arithmetic average of two instants. At a union cut, taking the upper middle rather than an average between two different central instants gives the same before/after record membership, because there is no observed instant between those central order statistics. A tied central instant stays intact under the registered strict-before/at-or-after rule. Missing times make the cut non-comparable; they never become zero.

Chronological pairing greedily takes the pair with the smallest absolute difference between those account medians, then removes that pair and repeats. Ties use the fixed hash of the sorted original account keys with the adapter's `ahas-pilot2-20260914:chronological:pair:` namespace. A separate fixed orientation hash selects A and B. This pairing occurs before any window grid or analyzer candidate is calculated. It does not optimize a boundary score. The paired-text adapter instead enumerates the three perfect matchings of each four-account stratum and uses its registered summed early/late median-gap rule, with arithmetic middle-pair medians.

`hash50` keeps records whose registered salted SHA-256 integer has even parity. Its retained count is approximately half, not exactly half. `middle50` removes canonical ordinals in `[floor(n/4), floor(3*n/4))`; odd-length effects follow those exact endpoints. Neither arm refills a unit. The prepared observations retain realized counts, words and time ranges.

The first paired candidate process loaded its plan immediately before the final additive missing-content clarification was registered. Its finalizer correctly refused that difference. That failed run remains historical. A fresh candidate pass uses the exact registered file in a new directory; it must reproduce the candidate-pool bytes and selected cohort observations before finalization. A completed leakage audit may be reused only through its identical candidate-pool hash. This is a repeat of preparation, not a relabeling of the initial execution.

The content audit has a recorded 3 GiB process address-space ceiling for these local runs, in addition to its registered two-million-candidate-pair cap. Failure to finish produces `not_auditable`, never successful singleton groups or a partial purge. This operational ceiling does not change a comparison rule or production resource limit.
